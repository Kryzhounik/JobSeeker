LANGUAGE REQUIREMENT WORDING: MARKET SAMPLE
============================================

Status: research note, not an executable filter configuration.

Scope
-----

The source sample contains 54 unique Senior Java / Senior Backend vacancies
from Poland, Ukraine, Moldova, and remote Europe, mostly recent vacancies from
2026. Mirrors of the same vacancy on different aggregators were excluded where
possible. Several vacancies contained more than one wording paired with an
explicit CEFR level, resulting in 59 pairs of "wording -> explicit CEFR".

This is not a random statistical sample of the whole market. It is an internal
calibration sample for interpreting HR language in relevant vacancies.

Observed results
----------------

Wording     Pairs   B2 / B2+       C1 / C1+       B2-C1       Interpretation
very good      9    7 (78%)        2 (22%)        0           primarily B2/B2+
excellent     10    6 (60%)        4 (40%)        0           ambiguous B2+-C1
fluent        29   16 (55%)       10 (34%)        3 (10%)     ambiguous B2+-C1
good           6    3 (50%)        3 (50%)        0           weak signal
strong         5    3 (60%)        0               2 (40%)     usually B2/B2+

Key observations
----------------

1. "Fluent English" more often corresponded to B2/B2+ than C1.

Examples from the sample:

- EPAM: "Fluent English" paired with B2+.
- ITDS: "Fluent English" paired with B2.
- Xebia: "Fluent English" paired with B2.
- Link Group and Luxoft also use "Fluent English" for C1.
- LeoVegas explicitly writes "Fluent English (C1 level is a minimum)".

Therefore, "fluent" alone does not reliably distinguish B2 from C1.

2. "Very good English" was predominantly B2/B2+.

Examples from the sample:

- Xebia: "very good English (B2+)".
- PAYBACK: "Very good English ... at least B2".
- ALTEN: "Very good English - at least B2+".
- Some vacancies from Codibly and Edge One paired it with C1.

3. "Excellent English" was split between B2/B2+ and C1.

Examples from the sample:

- Trui: "Excellent English communication skills (B2+)".
- Upvanta: "excellent English communication" paired with B2.
- Tenarai: "Excellent English communication skills" paired with B2.
- Netguru: "Excellent English skills" paired with C1.

4. "Good English" carried little information by itself.

Examples from the sample:

- IDT: "Good English level - B2 or higher".
- Luxoft: "Good spoken English" paired with B2 Upper Intermediate.
- Another Luxoft Senior Java vacancy paired "Good English" with C1 Advanced.

5. "Strong English" generally corresponded to B2/B2+.

Examples from the sample:

- EPAM: "Strong English communication at B2 level or higher".
- Next Ventures: "Strong English communication skills (B2+)".
- PTT: "Strong English communication (B2/C1 level)".

Suggested semantic calibration
------------------------------

HR wording                         Suggested interpretation without explicit CEFR
communicative English              B1/B1+
intermediate                       B1
good English                       B1+/B2; usually a general working requirement
upper-intermediate                 B2
very good English                  B2/B2+
strong English                     B2/B2+
excellent English                  B2+/C1
fluent English                     B2+/C1
advanced English                   C1 when used as a named proficiency level
professional working proficiency   B2
full professional proficiency      C1
near-native                        C1+/C2
native/bilingual proficiency       C2/native-like
proficient                         unclear without context
C2 Proficiency                     C2 when used as the formal qualification name

Interpretation notes
--------------------

The official CEFR descriptions do not reserve fluency for C1. A B2 speaker can
interact with sufficient fluency and spontaneity for regular communication.
C1 describes a broader, more consistent, and less effortful form of fluency.

Practical evidence-based conclusion: when no explicit CEFR level is stated,
"very good", "strong", "excellent", and "fluent" should not be treated as
reliable proof of a level above B2.

Current Seeker policy
---------------------

Despite that evidence, Seeker currently keeps "fluent" at C1. This is a
deliberately stricter product decision: mapping it to B2 would make almost all
vacancies pass the language filter because explicit C1/C2 wording is uncommon.
Do not silently change this mapping based only on the semantic calibration
above. Re-evaluate it together with the desired selectivity of the filter.

Explicit requirements such as "C1", "C1 minimum", or "English C1" remain
unambiguous and should take precedence over descriptive wording.
