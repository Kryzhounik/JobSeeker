# Technology proficiency levels

Shared proficiency scale for vacancy requirements and candidate technology
levels. For a vacancy, the rank describes the depth needed; for a candidate,
it describes the depth supported by confirmed experience.

| Rank | Normalized vacancy label | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | familiar | Familiarity: has tried the technology, knows the basics, and has completed isolated small tasks. |
| 2 | junior | Basic: can perform simple tasks but needs help with more complex work. |
| 3 | regular | Independent: can independently solve typical working tasks with the technology. |
| 4 | advanced | Advanced: can solve complex and unusual tasks, design solutions, and diagnose problems. |
| 5 | master | Expert: deeply understands capabilities, limitations, and internal mechanisms, and can investigate the most complex cases. |

- Requirement importance is separate from proficiency. Optional or
  nice-to-have wording sets `requirement`, not the proficiency rank.
- Duration, recency, and commercial or personal-project context are separate
  evidence. They do not automatically determine a rank; old experience is not
  automatically rank 1, and two years of experience is not automatically rank 3.
- A candidate's primary technology is not automatically rank 5.
- Use ranks 1-5 for listed technologies; do not enumerate technologies with no
  confirmed experience as rank 0.
